User direction (2026-10-12). This crowd is close to being used to train a crowd-avoiding
humanoid policy, and one behavior problem remains. Humans wait too long and are not
decisive. Two humans frequently bump into each other and stop, and then neither does
anything. Humans should still follow shortest-path principles and deviate minimally
from their existing path. The user suggested two directions, and either or both may be
right:
- additional transitions, for example a step back when a collision is detected;
- more intelligent transitions, for example deviating by a turn of the smallest angle
  that clears the other person.
The probability of avoidant behavior should rise. The population of stubborn,
non-avoidant humans should be low, but it must remain and stay easily tunable, because
the robot policy must learn to handle such humans.

The design today (src/crowdmotion/behavior/encounter.py; rules in
docs/usage/behavior-iteration.md under behavior designs). Humans follow random
waypoints and detect threats in a collision bubble. A cruising human switches with
probability 1 - exp(-rate dt), where rate = switch_rate_hz (1 - assertiveness) urgency.
Assertiveness is uniform on (0, 0.9) per human and decays as exp(-blocked/patience_s)
while the stop rule blocks the human. The human keeps the drawn intervention (sidestep,
veer, pivot or yield) until it finishes, and stops before colliding with a 1 cm target
clearance. A human that is already blocked cannot draw yield. Veer and pivot angles are
drawn from fixed ranges (20-45 and 45-90 deg), not computed from the threat.

Hypotheses visible in the code. They are not established; confirm or reject each from
traced standoff examples before changing behavior:
- Veer phase 0 travels along the current heading, which points at the threat, so the
  stop rule removes that motion and the veer stalls until its timeout.
- The sidestep's forward component and the veer arc keep pushing into the stop rule,
  so a blocked human that does decide still cannot move.
- Both humans yield, and both resume into each other.
- An intervention finishes, the cruise turn back toward the goal re-forms the
  conflict, and the cycle repeats.
- The graph does not realize a pivot or a backward step in time from standing.
- The threat test needs approaching intended velocities. Stopped or waiting humans, and
  humans whose intended motion is removed, may stop counting as threats, so no switch
  happens.
- Uniform assertiveness up to 0.9 and a 3 s patience make many humans effectively
  stubborn for several seconds.
