Focus of this run: the transitions between motions. The user reviewed the previous
candidates. Motion is much more realistic, but (a) sidestepping is too common compared
with turning the body (for example about 90 degrees) and then walking forward; (b) many
walking clips are visibly hunched while others are upright, and a human's posture should
not switch arbitrarily; (c) some clips change the human's vertical position in ways that
are infeasible on flat ground; (d) the user wants to tune the stochasticity live in
Viser. Determining the right transition behavior is the main job. Work in this priority
order, and record any item you do not finish as unfinished:

1. Edge annotations at load time. Derive per-edge labels from the saved tables
   (edge_pose height appears to be absolute height above the ground; verify it).
   This is not a graph rebuild: keep graph.pt and its format unchanged. hunch_ratio is
   baked into the tables and raises if overridden, so new labels, filters and costs need
   new GraphConfig fields that default off. Context measured on 2026-10-05 over the
   134,820 valid edges, to be verified before you rely on it: the head-height ratio to
   neutral_head_height (1.684 m, probably a T-pose) has median 0.935, with 21% of edges
   below 0.90. Hips-to-Neck trunk pitch from vertical has median 13 degrees, quartiles
   7/19 and p95 28. The head's forward offset from the hips has median 0.20 m. Pitch
   correlates -0.62 with the head ratio and 0.93 with the head offset, and neither
   tracks edge speed. About 62% of pitch variance lies between library streams, so
   posture is largely a property of the stream, and natural successors may already
   provide much of the persistence. Choose and validate a posture score: compare the
   candidate measures and inspect still contact sheets of sampled edges from each class
   before trusting any cutoff. Report the distribution and the reviewed examples rather
   than inventing a calibration.

2. Vertical feasibility on flat ground. Context: the Hips height range within an edge
   has p99 0.10 m and maximum 0.50 m; about 1% of edges keep the lower foot above
   0.18 m for the whole edge; one edge has a 1.8 m foot-height outlier. Choose an
   opt-in mask from the distribution plus visual review of flagged edges, and report how
   many edges and boundaries it removes. Terrain such as stairs is later work; do not
   design for it now. Masking can leave a boundary with no admissible candidate (all
   scores -inf, NaN in the Gumbel selection): count those dead ends and implement an
   explicit, tested fallback. Add executed-trace metrics for pelvis height and
   lowest-foot height to evaluation or diagnostics.

3. Per-human posture style. Each human carries a posture latent (at least upright
   versus hunched). It is sampled at reset from a configurable initial distribution
   whose default is strongly biased toward upright, and it evolves as a sticky
   stochastic process with configurable switching rates: hunched humans mostly stay
   hunched and upright humans mostly stay upright. The initial distribution applies at
   reset; switching rates apply live. The graph scores candidates against the latent
   with a soft cost term (a weight, not a hard filter, unless the joint coverage audit
   shows that a filter keeps enough walking and turning). start_boundaries must draw
   from the sampled class, so the start pose does not work against the latent. Use
   per-human core.rng streams under a new named human_seeds scheme; do not change the
   legacy plaza shared RNG. First audit posture class jointly with motion class,
   especially upright forward walking, upright forward turning and upright turning in
   place (earlier evidence: very few slow-turn edges; committed turns completed only
   8/49 attempts). Measure the realized posture share and switch rate against the
   configured values.

4. Turn then walk versus sidestep. Ordinary direction changes should mostly be
   realized by a body turn followed by forward walking. Sideways and backward travel
   should be uncommon in ordinary travel and remain available for deliberate or abrupt
   close avoidance. Make the rarity a tunable stochastic parameter (for example a
   per-human sidestep propensity or a cost weight), not a hard ban. Add a metric that
   classifies each ordinary direction change above a stated angle as body turn then
   forward, or as lateral/backward travel, and report it with the forward, sideways and
   backward shares relative to body facing. Curved forward paths are not sidesteps. If
   upright turning coverage limits this, report the evidence rather than hiding it.

5. Live tuning in Viser. Last, and time-boxed. Add a panel to crowd demo viser (in
